EVS Problem Overview

Understanding the Problem

Floods are among the most frequent natural disasters affecting many parts of North-East India. Heavy monsoon rainfall, overflowing rivers, mountainous terrain, land-use changes, and other environmental factors can increase flood vulnerability. Floods can affect human settlements, agriculture, forests, wildlife, infrastructure, and water quality.

🌧️

Natural Factors

Heavy monsoon rainfall, river overflow, high silt loads from the Himalayas, and distinctive geographical conditions such as narrow valley funnels and high drainage density create natural conditions for seasonal flooding.

🌲

Environmental Factors

Deforestation in catchment areas, rapid soil erosion, steep slope instability, land-use changes, wetland degradation, and ecosystem disturbance reduce the natural capacity of the land to absorb and regulate surplus water.

Deforestation Soil Erosion Wetland Loss
🏠

Human Impact

Damage to homes, village roads, schools, standing paddy crops, farm livestock, livelihoods, and public infrastructure. Repeated flood cycles create profound socio-economic stress for vulnerable rural communities.

Core Environmental Studies Theme

Environmental Impact of Floods

Floods alter fragile ecosystems, disrupt wildlife migrations, degrade fertile topsoil, and impact rural livelihoods. Understanding these multi-dimensional ecological consequences is the central mission of our EVS project.

🌾 Submerged Cropland Sali Paddy Inundation

🌱 Agriculture

Floodwater can damage crops, agricultural land, and soil, affecting food production and livelihoods. Standing water drowns monsoon paddy (Sali rice), causes waterlogging, and leaves thick sand deposits (sand-casting) that render fields uncultivable for years.

🌾 EVS Factor: Loss of agrarian livelihoods and soil salinization.
🌳 Riparian Forest Bank Erosion Zone

🌳 Forests

Flooding and associated erosion can disturb forest ecosystems and natural habitats. Riparian tree stands get uprooted by torrential currents, leading to severe riverbank slicing, loss of forest canopy, and altered forest succession dynamics.

🪵 EVS Factor: Riverbank collapse and biodiversity habitat shrinkage.
🦏 Kaziranga Sanctuary Karbi Anglong Refuge

🐘 Wildlife

Floods can disrupt animal habitats and force wildlife to move to safer areas. In Kaziranga National Park and Pobitora, endangered One-Horned Rhinoceroses, Asian elephants, and tigers must cross National Highway 715 to seek refuge in Karbi Anglong hills, exposing them to road collisions and poaching.

🦏 EVS Factor: Endangered species displacement & highway corridor threats.
💧 Silt & Contamination Turbidity > 800 NTU

💧 Water Quality

Floodwater can carry sediments, waste, and pollutants into rivers and other water sources. Inundated tube wells and municipal drains spread pathogens, triggering post-flood outbreaks of waterborne diseases like cholera, dysentery, and acute gastroenteritis.

🧪 EVS Factor: Potable water contamination and public health crises.
🏠 Marooned Settlements Char Community Displaced

🏠 Human Settlements

Floods can damage houses, roads, bridges, schools, and other infrastructure. Millions of people in riverine chars (sandbars) and lowlands face displacement, disruption of children's education, severed road transport links, and prolonged life in makeshift relief camps.

⛺ EVS Factor: Climate refugees, shelter destruction & supply bottlenecks.
🌍 Wetland Beels Ecological Equilibrium

🌍 Ecosystems

Flooding can alter landscapes and disturb the balance of local ecosystems. While natural flood pulses recharge wetlands (beels) and replenish fisheries, excessive unnatural sediment surges cause wetland siltation, invasive weed spread (water hyacinth), and river course braiding.

🔁 EVS Factor: Dual role of floods: ecological renewal vs catastrophic siltation.
Remote Sensing Technology

Seeing Floods From Space

Satellite imagery allows large areas of Earth's surface to be observed remotely. During flood events, satellite images can help researchers and authorities examine changes in water coverage and identify areas that may have been affected.

Why Traditional Ground Surveys Fall Short

When massive floods strike the Brahmaputra or Barak valleys, washed-out highways, submerged bridges, and dangerous river currents make manual ground-level assessment slow and perilous.

Earth observation satellites capture panoramic, georeferenced images across thousands of square kilometers simultaneously, providing continuous objective data for disaster mitigation.

📷 Optical Satellites
(e.g., Sentinel-2, Landsat-8) Capture natural color and infrared bands (NDWI). Highly detailed, but cannot pierce through heavy monsoon clouds.
📡 Radar / SAR Satellites
(e.g., Sentinel-1 SAR) Transmit active microwave pulses that penetrate clouds, haze, and rain day and night. The gold standard for flood mapping!

🛰️ How Satellites "See" Water: The Physics

  • 01. Specular Reflection: Calm open water acts like a smooth mirror, bouncing radar signals away from the satellite antenna. Thus, water appears deep black or dark blue.
  • 02. Diffuse Scattering: Trees, vegetation, and buildings have rough surfaces, scattering radar signals back to the sensor. They appear bright and textured.
  • 03. Change Detection: By comparing pre-flood and during-flood images, any pixel that turned from bright (land) to dark (water) is marked as inundated.

Technological Value

Why This Approach Matters

Combining spaceborne remote sensors with machine intelligence fundamentally transforms how society and governments prepare for and adapt to flood crises.

⚡

Faster Assessment

Satellite-based analysis can help assess large areas more quickly. Traditional field surveys can take weeks to reach remote villages; AI can process a multi-band satellite tile in seconds.

🛰️

Large-Area Monitoring

Satellite imagery can cover regions that may be difficult to access on the ground. Remote Himalayan foothill valleys and isolated river islands can be surveyed without putting rescue crews at risk.

🗺️

Visual Mapping

Detected areas can be represented visually on maps. Heatmaps and vector polygon boundaries clearly demonstrate which specific roads, bridges, and villages are submerged.

🌱

Environmental Monitoring

Flood information can help researchers understand environmental impacts, monitoring soil erosion hotspots, wildlife migration corridors in Kaziranga, and post-flood wetland recovery.

🚑

Disaster Management

Information about affected areas can support planning and response. State disaster management agencies (like ASDMA and NDRF) can prioritize evacuation boats, medical aid, and relief camps.

📊

Data-Based Decisions

Analysis can provide useful information for decision-makers. Objective historical flood data aids long-term urban planning, zoning regulations, and climate resilience investments.

Scientific Rigor

Challenges & Limitations

An honest evaluation of technological limitations is essential for academic credibility in Environmental Studies and Engineering.

Important Viva Distinction: AI and satellite systems are powerful tools, but they cannot replace ground truth data or experienced environmental hydrologists. Acknowledging model blind spots is what makes our college project scientifically sound.
☁️

Cloud Cover & Optical Satellites

Cloud cover can affect optical satellite imagery. Peak monsoon downpours are accompanied by dense cloud decks that blind visible sensors like Landsat and Sentinel-2, requiring radar (SAR) fallback.

📊

Training Data Dependency

AI models require suitable training data. If an AI is trained on flat European floodplains, it may misclassify steep Himalayan gorges or flooded dense jungle canopies in North-East India.

🔍

Resolution vs. Small Structures

Detection accuracy depends on image quality. Public satellite sensors have spatial resolutions of 10m to 30m per pixel. Narrow flooded village alleys and small culverts cannot always be resolved.

⛰️

Complex Landscape Geometry

Different landscapes can make flood detection difficult. Steep mountain shadows cast by hills in Arunachal Pradesh or Nagaland can mimic the low radar backscatter of water, producing false positives.

⏱️

Satellite Revisit Latency

Satellite data may not always be available immediately. A satellite constellation may only pass over a specific district once every 2 to 5 days, potentially missing sudden 6-hour flash flood peaks.

🛡️

Ground Validation Required

AI predictions should be validated before being used for critical decisions. Ground truthing via local gauge stations, drones, and district administration reports is vital before life-critical actions.

Quick Viva Reference Guide (For Student Presenter)

Four core answers to explain with confidence when professors question this project:

1. Why is this an EVS project, not just a CSE project? Because the core objective is understanding how floods devastate agro-ecosystems, disrupt wildlife at Kaziranga, cause topsoil erosion, and contaminate water. AI is simply the tool we employ to observe and protect these ecosystems.
2. Why use Radar (SAR) instead of regular photos? North-East India floods during peak monsoons when heavy cloud cover blocks optical cameras. Radar microwaves pass through clouds and rain 24/7, making SAR indispensable.
3. How does the AI know it is water? Smooth water produces specular reflection, bouncing radar energy away from the sensor (appearing dark). The AI segmentation model classifies dark low-backscatter clusters and subtracts pre-existing river channels.
4. What is the real-world utility? It provides district administrations (like ASDMA) with objective inundated area calculations (in km²), maps flooded evacuation routes, and directs relief supplies where needed most.
